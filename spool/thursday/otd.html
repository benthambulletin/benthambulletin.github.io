On This Day

November 26, 1789 — The country kept its first national Thanksgiving, a Thursday set by George Washington in a proclamation dated October 3, after the House asked that a committee “wait upon the President” about a day of thanks. It didn’t become a yearly habit. Abraham Lincoln brought it back in 1863, following Washington’s example down to the date: his proclamation also came on October 3, and his Thanksgiving also fell on Thursday, November 26. Congress didn’t pin it to the fourth Thursday until 1941, after a public outcry over Franklin Roosevelt’s attempt to move it up a week to stretch the Christmas shopping season. This year the fourth Thursday is November 26 again.
