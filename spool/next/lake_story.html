The Warmest Lake Erie on Record for November
Lake Erie hit 60 degrees off Buffalo on Thursday, the warmest it has ever been this deep into November, WKBW reports; the old mark was 58. It was still 59 Friday morning, the National Weather Service says, with Lake Ontario at 58 off Rochester. Last week's record warm air did it.
What it means for winter. Lake-effect snow comes from bitter air crossing warm water, and the bigger the gap, the heavier the bands. A 60-degree lake is a loaded battery: the first real arctic blast could set off an unusually intense storm. Until that cold arrives, early bands tend to fall as rain or slush, and no long cold spell shows up in the forecast models yet, WBLK notes. A lake this warm also freezes late, or barely at all, which can keep lake-effect going deep into winter.
Where it lands. On southwest winds, Erie's bands hit the Southtowns and Chautauqua County hardest, Dunkirk and Fredonia included. North Tonawanda gets the big totals only when the wind lines up just so. The cover shows what that looked like on Nov. 14, 2014.
