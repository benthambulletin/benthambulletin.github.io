On This Day

November 28, 1925 — A 77-year-old fiddler named Uncle Jimmy Thompson sat down in front of a microphone at WSM, a Nashville radio station barely seven weeks old, and George D. Hay put him on the air for the first WSM Barn Dance. Hay had come from Chicago’s National Barn Dance and copied the idea: country music, live, on Saturday nights. Two years later, coming on right after a network broadcast of classical music and grand opera, Hay told listeners that from then on they would hear the “Grand Ole Opry,” and the name stuck. It passed its 4,000th straight Saturday night broadcast in 2002 and is still on the air.
