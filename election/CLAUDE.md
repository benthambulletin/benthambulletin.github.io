# The Ballot — /election/ refresh rules

`/election/` is a public tracker Garret shares by link: https://benthambulletin.github.io/election/. It is a standalone page: no mention of The Bentham Bulletin, the paper, or any link back to the home page anywhere on it (Garret, Oct 14). It only happens to live in this repo. One job: every morning through Nov 4, 2026, bring `election/data.json` up to date and rebuild.

## Files
- `election/data.json` — every number on the page. Each row keeps its `url` (source). Keys: `as_of`, `updated_at`, `election_day`, `early_voting` (dates, county hours), `bottom_line` (four items, `order`/`headline`/`detail`), `what_changed` (list of strings), `generic_ballot`, `approval`, `senate`, `senate_intro`, `control`, `ny_governor` (the row with `"headline": true` is the single bar; every row is listed under "Show the polls"), `ny_house`, `pa_bellwethers`, `statewide`, `statewide_url`, `your_ballot` (five towns — North Tonawanda, Orchard Park, Scottsville/Wheatland, Dunkirk, Fredonia/Pomfret — each a list of `office`/`race`/`url` items plus `early_voting.site`), `election_night`, `glossary`, `outlook` (`title`, `as_of`, `text` — two or three sentences of judgment, rewritten only when the picture changes; labeled "Judgment, not polling"), `how_to_read`, `mood` ("Why the mood is sour" on the Today tab — three economic facts from BEA/BLS with sources; update the wage/price numbers only when BLS releases new ones, otherwise leave it alone) (stable text, reword only if a line becomes untrue), `key_dates` (feeds the dates strip and Election Day hours), `history` (one row per day: date, generic_ballot, trump_net, dem_senate_odds, hochul_margin), `mode` (`polls` or `results`), `results`.
- `election/build.py` — renders `index.html` from data.json. Run `python3 election/build.py` from the repo root (optional args: a data file and an output file, for testing). Never type a number into build.py or index.html.

## Page structure (Oct 18 — four tabs, every fact on exactly one tab)
Sticky tab bar; the URL hash picks the tab (`/election/#senate`), switching is client-side, `#today` is the default.

- **House** — New York seats, the Pennsylvania four (one intro), New York governor (one bar).
- **My ballot** — Statewide, Your town (all closed), Election night, What the words mean, What could still change this, Last 3 days (from `history`), Sources.

## Rules
- **Write for someone who doesn't follow politics (Garret, Oct 18: "if I'm not understanding, nobody else will").** Every `what_changed` item is ONE plain sentence first — who leads whom, by about how much, which way it moved — with a name and a (D)/(R) on every person named. No pollster names, dates, sample sizes or "RCP average" in that first sentence; put them in a second sentence if they matter; the page shows everything after the first sentence in smaller grey type. Say "about 3 points", not "+3.3"; under 1 point is "tied". Never write a bullet a reader has to decode. A `margin` is only set when a nonpartisan poll or an average from the last ~3 weeks supports it; a lone partisan or stale poll goes in `note` and `margin` stays null, so the page says "No public poll yet" rather than claiming a lead. The race rows are rendered as plain sentences by build.py from the data; the `note` field is the only place for pollster detail and it is hidden behind "Show the polls", which appears only on races with a `margin`.
- **Sabato's Crystal Ball:** centerforpolitics.org refuses automated fetches. Read its rating changes through a dated secondary (Newsweek, The Hill, Political Wire, 270toWin's change log) and cite that; don't mark the row "not refreshed" if a secondary from the same week confirms it.
- **Oct 15 (Garret): readers are nervous.** The page's job is to keep them steady and informed, not to soothe or alarm. The steadiness line (deltas across `history`) is computed by build.py; never edit it by hand.
- **Oct 14 fact-check lesson:** a search snippet is not a source. The first build cited an approval table to Wikipedia that the page did not actually show, called three Senate races "no poll" when RCP listed polls days old, and cited a House rating to a site that said the opposite. Before writing a row, open the url and read the number on the page; before writing "no poll", check RCP's latest-polls list for that state.
- Never print a number without a dated source row in data.json. If a source can't be read today, keep yesterday's row and say so in `what_changed` ("RCP Senate average not refreshed today").
- **Prefer averages over single polls.** For every Senate row use the RealClearPolling race-page average (and Silver Bulletin's where readable) as the headline number; a single poll goes in `note`, not in `dem_pct`/`rep_pct`, unless no average exists. When the only recent poll is from a partisan or low-rated firm (InsiderAdvantage, Trafalgar, Rasmussen, co/efficient, McLaughlin, any campaign internal, any pollster marked (R) or (D)), say so in the row's `note` in plain words ("Republican-leaning pollster") so readers can weigh it. Never let a single such poll move a bottom-line sentence on its own.
- Margins are Democrat minus Republican. `cook_rating` uses Cook's words.
- Election night and after: see **Election night** at the end of this file. It replaces the morning routine from the first results run on Nov 3.
- The Your-ballot box is reference, not news: keep it one `office`/`race` line per item, every item sourced, and never pad a town that has nothing.
- **Party tags (Garret, Oct 18):** every candidate name on the page carries a colored (D)/(R). build.py does it for the Senate, governor and House rows; in free-text fields (`your_ballot[*].items[*].race`, `statewide`) write the tag yourself after every name — "Smith (R, inc.) vs Jones (D)" — and build.py colors it. Never leave a name without one.
- Don't touch anything outside `election/`.

## Writing rules (Oct 18 cleanup — keep the page consistent)
- **One margin format.** Race badges are whole points: "D +3", "R +3", "Tied" (under 1 point). Sentences say "about 3 points" or "tied". National tiles and the Last 3 days table keep one decimal ("D +8.9") because small daily moves matter there. Never "Marshall +0.3" or "Hochul +12" in prose — name the leader and say "by about N points".
- **Ratings are Cook's**, shown as the pill only; never repeat the rating in a sentence, and say "Cook" (not "forecasters") when a rating is mentioned in text. Display spelling is "Toss-up".
- **Red and blue mean party only.** Only (D)/(R)-style tags are colored; never put a pollster or source name in parentheses next to a candidate.
- **One fact, one tab.** The Pennsylvania rule of thumb lives on the House tab; the Senate math on the Senate tab; early-voting sites on My ballot (and in the Today banner during early voting only); definitions in What the words mean. Do not restate them in `what_changed`, `bottom_line`, `glossary` or `election_night`.
- **Section titles are plain nouns.** Office labels in `your_ballot` and `statewide` are spelled out: "Congress (District 26)", "State Senate (District 62)", "Assembly (Districts 140, 145)", "County Legislature (Districts 7–9)", "Proposition: …" — never SD-/AD-/LD-/"Leg.".
- Every candidate named in free text carries a (D)/(R) tag; use surnames when data.json has no first name.

### Plain English on Today (Garret, Nov 3: "Cook? I don't know who that is, nobody else will")
Nothing on the Today tab assumes the reader follows politics. No rater or pollster names there (no Cook, Sabato, Silver, RealClearPolling, Decision Desk HQ): say "forecasters", "a nonpartisan race handicapper", "the vote-counting service". ### Status words (one meaning each)
- **Called** — DDHQ or AP (via Fox) has declared a winner; for county/town races, the county board's count is complete or a news outlet reports a winner or a concession.
- **Leading** — a candidate is ahead with a meaningful share counted (roughly a third or more of the expected vote) and by more than a point.
- **Too close** — ahead by under a point, or the race-call source says too close to call.
- **Not yet** — little or no vote counted. Leave `leader` empty.
- Under a third counted but a lead over a point: **Too close** with `leader` filled (it shows as "edge"). Never set `leader` on a Not yet row.

### The `results` object (build.py renders it; do not touch build.py)
- `updated` — the ACTUAL wall-clock time you write it, read from the clock (`TZ=America/New_York date -Iminutes`), never the run's slot name and never a rounded or future time (Nov 3: a hand run stamped 9:45 while it was 9:37). ISO, Eastern, e.g. `2026-11-03T21:37:00-05:00`. The page header and "The count at" read it.
- `senate` / `house` — `{dem, rep, note, url}`. Senate counts are **total seats including those not up this year** (DDHQ balance of power, or AP via Fox). House is seats called so far. `note` is optional, one short phrase.
- `races` — pre-seeded rows; update in place, never delete or reorder. Fields: `key`, `group` (`senate` / `house` / `governor`), `race`, `candidates` (keep as is), `held_by`, `leader` ("Roy Cooper (D)" — name plus party tag; for a proposition "Yes" or "No"), `pct_lead` (percentage-point margin, a number), `status`, `reporting` ("About 80% counted"), `url` (the page you read). build.py computes the path to 51, flips and "Would flip" from `held_by`, `leader` and `status` — never write that math yourself.
- `local` — same fields plus `towns`.
- `next` — "What's next tonight": 2–4 items `{when, what}`, only things still ahead (poll closings, a state that usually reports late, Maine's ranked-choice count, Alaska's Nov 18 tabulation). Drop anything already past. Poll closings ET (greenpapers, read Nov 1; 270toWin, read Nov 3): most of IN, KY 6:00; GA, SC, VA, VT, most of FL 7:00; NC, OH 7:30; PA, ME, most of MI, TX, KS 8:00; NY, MN, IA, rest of MI/TX/KS 9:00; Alaska midnight, Aleutians 1 a.m.
- `headline` — ONE sentence, the biggest thing right now (Garret, Nov 3: the purple box at the top reports the headline and the next thing coming). E.g. "Roy Cooper (D) wins North Carolina, the first Senate flip of the night." Rewrite every run; (D)/(R) tags on names; no cheering.
- The box's **Next** line counts down live in the reader's browser: every `next` item that is a clock event carries `at` (ISO, Eastern, e.g. `2026-11-03T19:00:00-05:00`), and the page shows the first future one with "in 2h 11m 15s", rolling to the next on its own. Keep `next` in time order; give every timed item an `at`; items without `at` (a recount, "days later") are shown only in the timeline.
- `source_note` — one line naming where tonight's calls came from, in plain words ("Winners are called by Decision Desk HQ, a vote-counting service, and the Associated Press; local counts come from the county boards and are unofficial until certified."), plus any race that couldn't be read.
