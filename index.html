🍁The Sunday Spotlight🍁

The Month in Migraine
A quiet month for the medicines this family uses, with a few things worth knowing. An AI wrote this section.
1. Our own October. Seven migraines logged from Sept. 27 through Oct. 31: Garret four (Oct. 6, 23, 27 and 29), Mom two (Oct. 9 and 29), Dad one (Oct. 6). Twice, two of us logged on the same day: Garret and Dad on Oct. 6, Garret and Mom on Oct. 29. What it means: Garret's four came in two clusters, which fits what his own log has shown before: attacks tend to bunch together. Keep logging; the pattern only shows with the count.
2. Migraine and endometriosis travel together. A Mayo Clinic analysis of 35 studies covering about 13 million women, presented Oct. 20 to the American Neurological Association, found women with endometriosis (womb-lining tissue growing outside the womb) had about 2.3 times the odds of migraine. What it means: it's a link, not proof that one causes the other, but anyone with both kinds of pain should mention both to a doctor.
3. A new kind of rescue pill reaches the last round. TriGemX Bio started phase 3 trials, the final round of testing before an FDA application, for elismetrep, a pill that blocks TRPM8, a "cold-sensing" switch on the nerves of the face and head. What it means: nothing for this winter; first results are expected in mid-2027.
4. A small reassurance on the heart. A Swiss clinic followed 17 people with a past stroke, mini-stroke or heart disease who took CGRP-blocking migraine drugs for up to five years: no new strokes or heart problems, and 13 of the 17 cut their migraine days at least in half. What it means: encouraging, but 17 people with no comparison group is a very small study; call it modest.
Coming down the road. Lundbeck's bocunebart, which blocks a second migraine messenger called PACAP, has a late-stage update expected by year's end, and September's updated prevention guideline from neurologists and headache doctors names no single first-choice drug, leaving the pick to doctor and patient. And the clocks: standard time returned at 2 a.m. today; daylight saving time shifts an hour of daylight from morning to evening from March to early November, CBS explains. Any schedule change can nudge sleep, so the next few nights are worth guarding.

✶The National Wire✶

1

Update: Out of Oleshky

Residents evacuated from the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky crossed into Ukrainian-held territory Saturday after traveling by bus and plane through Russia and Belarus, CBS reports. The roughly 2,000 people left in the city were offered the choice of going to either side; the ceasefire runs through this evening.

2

Trump's Suit Against the Journal Tossed

A federal judge in Miami threw out President Trump's $10 billion defamation lawsuit against the Wall Street Journal and Rupert Murdoch over its story on his ties to Jeffrey Epstein, saying he was "grasping at straws," NPR reports. The dismissal is with prejudice, meaning the suit can't be refiled.

3

Valkyries Win in Their Second Year

The Golden State Valkyries won their first WNBA title Saturday, beating the Atlanta Dream 68–63 in Game 7, CBS reports. Gabby Williams scored 30 to cap a season that broke WNBA records for viewing and attendance.
