The News
The Lead

Brooklyn Building Collapses

A three-story apartment building on Flatbush Avenue in Brooklyn collapsed just after 4 p.m. Wednesday, killing at least six people and injuring 19, CBS reports. The side of the building next door was torn off as it fell.

People were living in the building at the time, police said, and the lot beside the two buildings was a construction site; people from both were trapped. More than 250 firefighters and medics responded, and the first fire company, already in the neighborhood, arrived in less than a minute.

The cause is under investigation.

The National Wire

1

A Democrat Wins in Mississippi

Democrat Scott Colom, a 43-year-old district attorney, defeated Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in a race that stayed up in the air for days, CBS projects. It is the eighth Senate seat Democrats have taken from Republicans this fall, and Colom will be Mississippi's first Black senator since Reconstruction, the years just after the Civil War.

2

A Great Liner Goes Under on Purpose

The SS United States, the 1,000-foot ocean liner that set a transatlantic speed record in 1952 that still stands, was deliberately sunk Wednesday about 20 nautical miles off Florida's Gulf Coast, CBS reports. She will become the world's largest artificial reef, a man-made home for fish and a draw for divers.

3

Found After Six Days in the Bush

A 17-year-old with autism who vanished on a camping trip east of Melbourne, Australia, was found alive Thursday, asleep on a flat rock beside a creek, by a police helicopter crew, CBS reports. He was sunburned but mostly healthy; "I'm just over the moon that he's alive," his mother said.

The Home Wire

1

Rochester · High Falls Park Breaks Ground

Work on the first phase of High Falls State Park begins with a groundbreaking Wednesday, Nov. 18, WXXI reports: High Falls Terrace, on the eastern edge of the gorge, with a Heritage Walk and Memorial Wall telling the stories of people who came to the Rochester area. The 85-acre park around the gorge would be the first state park in the city.

2

Hanover · A Memorial and a Time Capsule

The Chautauqua County town of Hanover dedicated its new Veterans Memorial on Wednesday and buried a time capsule to be opened July 4, 2076, the nation's 300th birthday, WKBW reports. "This memorial was built for you," Town Clerk Elizabeth VanCheri, who led the project, told the veterans there.

3

North Tonawanda · Lowry School Deal Falls Through

The plan for the county land bank to take over the empty Lowry Middle School and pass it to developers has fallen through, WKBW reports; the owners, 621 Payne LLC, say they will develop it themselves, as 88 senior apartments. The land bank's plan called for several dozen apartments in the school and lots for new homes where Vetter Stadium stood.

The Marquee

1

Anthony Clark of Yes, Dear Dies at 62

Anthony Clark, the comedian who starred in the CBS sitcom Yes, Dear, has died, his manager told Us Weekly; Deadline reports the cause was complications of pneumonia. Co-star Mike O'Malley, a longtime friend, announced the death.

2

Frankie Muniz Walks Away From a Crash

The Malcolm in the Middle star, who took up racing in 2023, was taken to a hospital Wednesday after a practice crash at Daytona International Speedway and released the same day, E! News reports. "I am a little sore," he said, sorry to miss the Veterans Day race he was there to run.

3

France Honors Nicole Kidman

Nicole Kidman will receive an honorary César, the highest award in French film, for her life's work at the ceremony in Paris in February, the Hollywood Reporter says. The French film academy called her "a Hollywood star and a major figure in auteur cinema," meaning films shaped by a director's personal vision.

4

The Sabres Draft a Dog

The Buffalo Sabres introduced this season's team dog: Ralph, an 8-month-old Samoyed named for Ralph Wilson, the Bills' original owner, WKBW reports. He will train to be a service dog for a local veteran through WNY Heroes' Pawsitive for Heroes program.
