The National Wire

The Lead

Kilmar Ábrego García Leaves for Spain

Kilmar Ábrego García, the Maryland man whose wrongful deportation to El Salvador last year became a flashpoint in the fight over President Trump’s immigration agenda, has left the United States for Spain, the BBC reports. He came to the U.S. illegally from El Salvador as a teenager and is married to a U.S. citizen.

The government admitted it had sent him to El Salvador by mistake and brought him back, but only to face human trafficking charges, which were later dropped, the BBC reports. The administration then kept trying to send him to other countries, including Uganda and Ghana.

Senator Chris Van Hollen, the Maryland Democrat who visited him in El Salvador’s Cecot prison in 2025, said in a statement Tuesday:

“Today, Kilmar is travelling to a country of his choice, a free man – unharmed and with dignity.”

Huawei Guilty of Stealing Trade Secrets

A federal jury in Brooklyn found the Chinese telecom giant Huawei guilty on 10 of 12 counts, including conspiracy to steal trade secrets, bank fraud and conspiracy to violate U.S. sanctions, after a two-month trial, the BBC reports. Jurors acquitted it of the most serious charge, racketeering conspiracy, which would have required finding the company acted like a criminal enterprise; Huawei said it would take all necessary legal action to defend itself.

3

Six Dead in Plane Crash off Mississippi

Six people, four adults and two boys aged 8 and 10, were found dead Tuesday after a small plane went down in the Gulf of Mexico between Pascagoula, Mississippi, and Horn Island, the Coast Guard says. The Cessna 210 was flying from New Orleans to Fort Myers, Florida, when air traffic control lost contact with it at about 3:45 p.m., CBS and the BBC report.

The Home Wire

Monroe County · Project Home

Monroe County wants to shift $2.8 million in public assistance money into Project Home, which gives bigger housing allowances, up to 60 percent of fair market rent, to people on public assistance who are most at risk of becoming homeless, WXXI reports. It would help up to 600 households, but it needs state approval.

4

Niagara Falls · Dispatcher Disciplined Over 911 Call

A Niagara Falls police dispatcher was disciplined in August over the handling of an emergency call reportedly placed about a half hour before a woman was fatally shot on Highland Avenue over the Fourth of July weekend, the Niagara Gazette reports. In a letter to the resident who complained, the department’s office of professional standards said the allegations, about “courtesy and communication responsibilities,” were sustained.

5

Fredonia · Back to Dunkirk for Water?

Two Fredonia village trustees, Tim Mulholland and LeeAnn Lazarony, want to halt the plan to get water from the North County Water District through Pomfret and instead revisit connecting directly to the city of Dunkirk, the Dunkirk Observer reports. The board approved a direct Dunkirk link in December 2023, but residents sued and stopped it; the Pomfret plan has since stalled over funding.

6

Chautauqua County · Ten Measles Cases

Chautauqua County has had 10 reported measles cases, the county’s Board of Health heard, and the state’s count hadn’t risen as of Tuesday morning, the Observer reports. The county’s public health director, Lacey Wilson, asks anyone who suspects measles to call a doctor first rather than go straight to the emergency room, to avoid exposing others.

“The Buffalo Miracle” Is on HBO

“The Buffalo Miracle,” a documentary about the people who helped one another through the deadly Christmas 2022 blizzard, debuted Tuesday night on HBO, WKBW reports. It’s from two-time Oscar-winning director Ben Proudfoot, with Josh Allen as an executive producer through his new production company; a suburban McDonald’s is the centerpiece.

Milton met her on a chance visit to a Jamestown candy shop; Alexandra Daddario spoke to students at the Nov. 15 premiere at the Milton Hershey School.

The New Turkey Trot

What changed. Through last year the race was a point-to-point run: it started at Delaware and Tacoma in North Buffalo, by the Target plaza at 2626 Delaware, ran straight down Delaware Avenue through Niagara Square, and turned onto Church and Franklin to finish by the Convention Center, with shuttles hauling runners back to their cars on Delaware. This year, the 131st, it's a single 8K loop (4.97 miles) that starts and finishes at the Convention Center. The YMCA says the move puts packet pickup, warm indoor space and restrooms at the start line.

The course. The start is on Franklin Street between West Chippewa and West Tupper. The first half heads north on the east side of Delaware, on streets the map lists as Main, Linwood and Goodell, up to West Ferry and Gates Circle. The second half is the part everybody remembers: back down Delaware Avenue to Niagara Square, then Church and Pearl to the finish at Franklin and West Eagle. For every turn, see the official route map.

A sketch, not to scale, with east-west stretched to fit. Landmarks per the YMCA's 2024 and 2026 race pages.

Getting there. Forget the Target lot; the shuttles from it are gone. The YMCA says parking and drop-offs are centered on the Convention Center, with shuttles from the KeyBank Center surface lots, and posts the lots on its runner page. Enter the Convention Center through the Franklin Street doors; everyone goes through security, and the other doors are exit only.

The plan for the six of you
  • Wednesday: pick up the packets at the Amherst YMCA, 150 Tech Drive, 10 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. (Race-morning pickup exists, 7 to 8:15 at the Convention Center, but that's a line you don't need.)
  • Thursday, 8:00 at the latest: parked and walking in. The YMCA says arrive at least an hour early.
  • 8:00 to 8:40: stay inside where it's warm; real bathrooms are inside too.
  • 8:40: out to Franklin and line up by the pace flags. Walkers go toward the back.
  • 9:00 sharp: the gun. There's no water on the course, only at the finish and inside.
  • After: the party is upstairs at the Convention Center. The alcohol-free family room is Room 106 on the lower level.
Margo's take

A hundred and thirty years of running down Delaware, and they moved the start so you can pee indoors. Fine. Nobody's going to miss standing at Delaware and Tacoma at 8 in the morning with the wind going right through them. But the Target lot was the whole damn ritual: park, freeze, run, ride a bus back to your car. Now it's a loop that starts where the beer is. Progress, I guess. It's on probation. — Margo

On This Day

November 25, 1950 — Thanksgiving weekend, a storm took shape over the Carolinas and became what the weather service calls the Great Appalachian Storm. It dropped 30.5 inches of snow on Pittsburgh and as much as 62 at Coburn Creek, West Virginia; Louisville, Lexington and Bowling Green set November record lows that day, and gusts hit 94 mph in New York City and 140 at Bear Mountain. By the time it spun out over Lake Erie and into Canada, 160 people were dead. Buffalo, on the warm side of it, got no snow at all and sat in the 40s while Pittsburgh shivered in the single digits.
