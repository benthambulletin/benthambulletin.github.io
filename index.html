The Lead

Voyager 1 Is About to Be One Light-Day Away

On Wednesday, NASA's Voyager 1 is set to reach a distance no spacecraft has reached: one light-day from Earth, NPR reports. A light-day is how far light travels in 24 hours, more than 16 billion miles. Nothing people have launched has gone farther.

Voyager 1 left Earth on Sept. 5, 1977, and has been flying for nearly 50 years. It passed Jupiter and Saturn on the way out, and in 1979 it discovered a faint ring system around Jupiter.

NASA wants company for the occasion. On Tuesday night it is asking people to blink the probe's call sign, V-G-R, at the sky in Morse code, with a flashlight, a phone light or anything else that flashes. V is three quick flashes and a long one. G is two long and one quick. R is quick, long, quick. NASA calls it a symbolic celebration.

A Third U.S. Carrier Reaches the Middle East

The aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt has joined two other American carriers in the Middle East as the war with Iran goes on, a U.S. military official confirmed to CBS; the three-carrier presence is expected to be temporary as ships rotate through. Bahrain said it intercepted Iranian strikes, and Iran's Foreign Ministry on Sunday rejected Germany's demand that it stop enriching uranium, the process that makes nuclear fuel.

2

Five Million Fewer on Food Aid

About five million people have lost federal food benefits over the past year, since a sweeping tax and spending law cut funding for SNAP, the program once known as food stamps, and tightened who qualifies, NPR reports. It is considered the fastest decline in decades; in San Diego, workers at one county benefits office spent Saturday morning loading groceries into people's cars.

3

Mexico's "Boss of Bosses" Dies at 80

Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo, who ran the Guadalajara Cartel at the height of the 1980s cocaine boom, has died in a hospital at 80, the BBC reports. He is credited with building an alliance between Colombian traffickers and his own cartel, a way of doing business the BBC says endures to this day.

Rochester · $60 Million for the Old Kodak Park

New York will put $60 million toward modernizing the power grid at Eastman Business Park, the 1,200-acre former Kodak Park, Gov. Kathy Hochul announced, WXXI reports. The whole project is estimated at more than $93 million and is meant to bring enough electricity to empty buildings and open lots to attract new manufacturers.

2

Update: Wilson Wins It All

The Wilson girls soccer team won the program's first state championship Sunday in Dryden, finishing a perfect 21–0 season, the Observer reports. The Lakewomen outscored their opponents 12–1 across the state tournament.

3

Dunkirk · High School Hoops on an NBA Floor

The Dunkirk and Forestville boys basketball teams will play each other Dec. 13 in the Cleveland Cavaliers' home arena, Dunkirk athletic director Kyle Jarrell told the school board, the Observer reports. It is part of a series the Cavaliers run for high school teams; the junior varsity game tips off at 11 a.m.

An Oscar for Glenn Close, at Last

Glenn Close received an honorary Oscar on Sunday night at the Governors Awards, the film academy's annual dinner for career honors, the Hollywood Reporter says. Animator Floyd Norman and director Ridley Scott, who was absent after a recent medical procedure, were honored too.

Grammy Nominations This Morning

The nominees for the 2027 Grammy Awards are being announced on a livestream starting at 7:45 this morning, Billboard reports. Olivia Dean, the reigning best new artist, is widely expected to land several of the top ones, and she is one of the presenters reading the names.

A Fourth Baby Mahomes

Brittany Mahomes, wife of Chiefs quarterback Patrick Mahomes, was at Sunday's game in Atlanta five days after announcing she is expecting the couple's fourth child, E! News reports. They have three children: Sterling, Bronze and Golden.
