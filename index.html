☑The Ballot · Results☑

Democrats Take Congress
The Senate. Democrats have won it. They took seven Republican-held seats: Troy Jackson beat Susan Collins in Maine (Collins conceded), Sherrod Brown beat Jon Husted in Ohio, James Talarico beat Ken Paxton in Texas, Josh Turek won Iowa, Adam Hamilton beat Roger Marshall in Kansas, Roy Cooper won North Carolina and Annie Andrews won South Carolina. They kept every seat they had to defend, including Michigan, where Abdul El-Sayed beat Mike Rogers. The AP count at 12:30 a.m. was 52 to 41, before Maine and Michigan were called; Alaska and Nebraska are still counting.
The House. Democrats are winning it. They flipped three Pennsylvania seats, with Bob Brooks, Paige Cognetti and Janelle Stelson beating Republican incumbents, and in New York's Hudson Valley, Mike Lawler (R) conceded NY-17 to Cait Conley (D). News outlets say Democrats have the majority; the AP had them at 197 seats to 117 at 12:30 a.m., with 121 seats still uncalled.
New York. Hochul (D) beat Bruce Blakeman (R) for a second full term; the AP called it at 9:18 p.m.
Our House seats. NY-26 (North Tonawanda): Kennedy (D) leads Hannon (R) 65 to 34 percent with about 82 percent counted. NY-23 (Orchard Park, Dunkirk, Fredonia): Langworthy (R) won, the AP called. NY-25 (Scottsville): Morelle (D) leads McIntyre (R) 63 to 37 percent with about 75 percent counted.
Albany. In North Tonawanda's open Assembly seat, Wydysh (R) leads McMurray (D) with 54 percent, unofficially. For Scottsville, Helming (R) leads Mills (D) for State Senate, and Hawley (R) was reported reelected to the Assembly. Ortt's and Borrello's Senate seats, the other Assembly races, North Tonawanda's community-center vote and the county races aren't in the tracker yet.
What it means. From January, a Democratic Congress and a Republican president: new laws will need both, and Democrats will chair the committees. What's next: Westchester's delayed ballots are counted today to settle NY-17; Alaska's final tally is Nov. 18, when New York counties also begin the official canvass.

✶The National Wire✶

1

Guilty Plea in an Ethiopia Spy Case

Abraham Teklu Lemma, a former U.S. contractor born in Ethiopia, pleaded guilty to passing dozens of classified documents and photos to senior Ethiopian officials over more than two years, the Justice Department said, the BBC reports. He hoped some of it would help target fighters from Ethiopia's Tigray region during the 2020–22 civil war; under the plea deal he faces up to 22 years in prison.

2

A Cruise Ship Sails On, Empty

The MV Odyssey, a Florida company's ship on a planned three-year trip around the world, left Cambodia late Tuesday with only crew aboard, CBS reports. The company suspended the voyage last week over soaring fuel costs and gave its 203 passengers a few hours' notice to get off at the port of Sihanoukville.

⛪The Home Wire⛪

1

Rochester · Campus Hacking Charges

Two Canadian men are accused of a computer-hacking scheme aimed at schools and colleges, including some in Western New York, the U.S. Attorney's office says, Rochester First reports. Investigators say they ran the scheme through private servers in several countries, registered under false names.

2

Fredonia · Honoring Student Veterans

SUNY Fredonia holds its annual Veterans Day ceremony Friday at 3:30 p.m. in the Williams Center Multipurpose Room, open to the public, the Observer reports. Veterans and students will speak, and more than $3,700 in scholarships will go to students connected to the veteran community.

3

Pomfret · Recycling Yard Still Waiting

After three meetings, Pomfret's zoning appeals board still hasn't granted Steve St. George a permit for a concrete and asphalt recycling yard on Webster Road, on land that used to grow grapes, the Observer reports. He needs a special-use permit, a sign-off for a business the zoning doesn't allow on its own.

★The Marquee★

1

Tokyo's Top Prize to a Manhole Cover

Milcho Manchevski's Sister Brother Manhole Cover, a comic fable about two siblings who strip scrap metal from the streets of Skopje, North Macedonia, won the Grand Prix at the Tokyo International Film Festival, Variety reports. The prize comes with about $19,000, Deadline adds.

2

Australia Starts a National Songbook

Midnight Oil's "Beds Are Burning" and the Easybeats' "Friday on My Mind" are among the first 10 songs named to Australia's new National Songbook, announced Wednesday in Canberra, Billboard reports. The yearly honor recognizes songs "whose creative vision has given voice to Australian stories."

3

Van Damme Picks Up a Sword

Jean-Claude Van Damme will star in Katana, a yakuza action film set in Japan, Deadline reports. It's from French producer Jeremy Zag, the creator of the cartoon hit Miraculous.
