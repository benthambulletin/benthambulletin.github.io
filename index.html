The Lead

Every Departure in the Country Was Held for Hours

The Federal Aviation Administration ordered a nationwide ground stop on every departing flight in the United States Friday afternoon, an order that holds planes at the gate instead of letting them take off, citing "extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety," CBS reports. The BBC puts the announcement at 3:54 p.m. Eastern and calls it the longest nationwide ground stop since Sept. 11, 2001.

Officials blamed a telecommunications outage. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed "an incredibly old air traffic control system" whose routers "got overloaded with data," and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no sign of a cyberattack. Sources told CBS that an update to the private network controllers use set off a reboot and a crash.

"At 8:15 tonight, Eastern time, we've opened up the airspace. … It's going to be slow."
— Sean Duffy

FlightAware counted more than 8,500 delayed flights and nearly 8,000 cancellations Friday, according to the BBC. More than 3,500 planes were in the air when the order came; some were diverted and others flew on to where they were going. "It's going to take us some time to unwind what's happened this afternoon," Duffy said.

In Rochester, most Friday evening departures and arrivals were cancelled, and Saturday's flights were listed on time as of late Friday, WXXI reports; Buffalo's airport was disrupted too, per WGRZ. Anyone flying this weekend should check with the airline before leaving for the airport. Airlines owe passengers on a cancelled flight a free rebooking or a refund, WXXI notes.

Tanker Hits a Docked Riverboat in New Orleans

A tanker, the Atlantic Asphalt, struck the docked paddlewheel cruise boat American Heritage about 12:30 p.m. local time Friday near the Port of New Orleans, leaving the riverboat on its side and largely underwater, CBS reports. Everyone was accounted for and no one died; Mayor Helena Moreno said 32 passengers were hospitalized, none in critical condition. The Coast Guard's Capt. Melanie Burnham said, "There seems to be some type of a loss of steering," according to the BBC.

2

Trump, Putin and Xi to Sit Down Together

President Trump, Russia's Vladimir Putin and China's Xi Jinping will hold the first three-way meeting among them Thursday in China, on the sidelines of an Asia-Pacific economic summit, the White House said Friday, according to CBS. Trump wants to talk about cutting nuclear arsenals and plans to raise the war in Iran, but Russia's war in Ukraine is not expected to come up, officials said.

3

Starship Reaches Orbit; Its Booster Does Not Come Home

SpaceX's Starship, the world's biggest rocket, reached orbit Friday on its 15th test flight, but the booster that was supposed to fly back and be caught at the Texas launch pad aborted automatically and ended up in the Gulf of Mexico, PBS reports. The spacecraft itself is to steer back toward the pad today for a first attempt at catching it, barring further trouble.

4

Update: Microsoft Is Back

Microsoft said early Saturday that Outlook, Teams and Copilot are fully restored after the worldwide outage that began Thursday, the BBC reports. The outage reached more than 70 countries, and the company apologized.

The Home Wire

1

Chautauqua County · Runners at the State Meet Today

Chautauqua County runners race this morning at the state cross country championships at Queensbury High School, the Observer reports. The Clymer/Sherman/Panama boys, at the meet as a varsity team for the first time, are seeded first in Class D, the small-school division, in some pre-meet projections, coach Ray Shrout said.

2

Niagara-Wheatfield · Coach "Ace" Cacciatore, 91

Armand "Ace" Cacciatore, the Niagara-Wheatfield High School football and wrestling coach whose career ran four decades, died Wednesday at 91, the Niagara Gazette reports. The paper calls him one of the most successful and respected high school coaches in Western New York history, known to generations of athletes simply as "Coach."

3

Niagara Falls · The Falls by Helicopter, After Dark

Rainbow Air Helicopter Tours was set to begin night flights over Niagara Falls on Friday, the first in its 32 years, the Niagara Gazette reports. The tours run Friday and Saturday evenings from 5 to 8:30 through March 13.

Journey's Drummer Steps Away

Deen Castronovo, Journey's longtime drummer, is leaving the band's farewell tour "to focus on personal health matters," the group announced Friday, Billboard reports. Steve Murphy, a New York drummer who has played with Trans-Siberian Orchestra, fills in for the rest of November.

2

The Hunger Games Author Speaks Up

Suzanne Collins, who wrote The Hunger Games and stays out of the spotlight, sent fans a rare message Friday, shared on the movie's Instagram, praising the coming movie of her prequel, Sunrise on the Reaping, E! News reports. She called director Francis Lawrence's film "a vibrant and devastating adaptation of the novel."

3

Tonight on TV: Mulaney Hosts SNL

John Mulaney hosts Saturday Night Live tonight, TVLine reports. Also on tonight: the singer Alex Warren hosts Nickelodeon's Kids' Choice Awards, and Hallmark has a new movie, The Nights Before Christmas.

The Gridiron

Bills: Dawkins and Anderson Back

Offensive linemen Dion Dawkins and Alec Anderson, who both missed Monday's game in Minnesota, practiced in full Friday and are set to play Sunday, and so is Connor McGovern, who has been dealing with a neck injury, Buffalo Rumblings reports. Right tackle Spencer Brown is doubtful with an ankle injury. "This is a division game on the road, it's going to be as hard as it gets, right?" first-year coach Joe Brady said, according to the Observer.

Jets: Wilson Expected Back

Coach Aaron Glenn said receiver Garrett Wilson has one step left in the concussion protocol, the league's checklist before a player can return from a head injury, and that he is confident Wilson will clear it and play against Buffalo, ProFootballTalk reports. Wilson missed last week's game; he has 43 catches for 527 yards and three touchdowns this season.
