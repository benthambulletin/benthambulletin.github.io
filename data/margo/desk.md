# From Margo's Desk

## Wed Nov 25, 2026 — pitch (sent Mon Nov 23, 8:30 p.m.)

**Option 1 — Democrats won big. Now what?**
- Why now: NPR's count (Nov 19) has Democrats winning House races 56% to 42%, the biggest win for either party in 50 years, with 46 seats flipped and up to 260 seats if they win the races they lead, their largest House majority since 1991–93. https://www.opb.org/article/2026/11/19/democrats-house-win-was-the-largest-in-50-years/
- Margo's angle: spend the majority fast on the kitchen table (prices, health costs, housing) instead of a two-year hearing marathon; waves like 2006 and 2018 faded.
- Case for investigations first: oversight is Congress's job, the voters who delivered the wave expect accountability, and checking the executive is the one thing a House majority can do on its own.
- Case for legislating first: bills need the Senate and a signature, so a House that only investigates gets blamed for the gridlock; midterm waves have shrunk before (2006, 2010, 2018), and prices are what moved voters.

**Option 2 — Are the big climate summits worth the jet fuel?**
- Why now: COP31 in Turkey ended Sunday without a major deal after the longest talks ever held; the final text never mentioned fossil fuels (BBC, Nov 22). Closer to home: Lake Erie is still 54 degrees in late November and none of our four towns has measured snow yet.
- Margo's angle: thirty years of summits and the hot air is the only thing that's measurably increased; the real action is cheap clean power and what states and towns build.
- Case for the summits: they're the only room where small and poor countries face the big emitters, and the Paris pledges came out of one; quitting hands the agenda to whoever stays.
- Case against: every country gets a veto, so one oil producer can gut the text; emissions have kept climbing through 30 of them.
- Sources: BBC via the paper's Nov 23 feed; NPR on COP30's similar ending, https://www.npr.org/2025/11/22/nx-s1-5615207/u-n-climate-talks-end-cop30-brazil

Garret's pick (Mon 9:00 p.m.): **Option 1**, "your angle is spot on": spend the majority fast on prices, health costs and housing, not two years of hearings. Draft due Tuesday night for his yes.
