- **Sun Oct 4, 2026 — Sunday Spotlight: the month in migraine.** Say in the section that an AI wrote it. Items, plain English, verified through Sept 27: (1) **New national prevention rules.** On Aug 31 the American Academy of Neurology and the American Headache Society published their first new migraine-prevention guideline since 2012. The daily pill Garret takes (Qulipta, generic name atogepant — a "CGRP blocker," which stops a nerve chemical that sets off attacks) is on the top tier of drugs doctors should offer, alongside the monthly-shot versions of the same idea and the older blood-pressure and seizure pills; patients no longer have to fail an old drug first. The guideline also says a preventive that is working should be *discussed* at six months, not automatically stopped, and that price is a fair tiebreaker (https://www.aan.com/PressRoom/Home/PressRelease/5361 ; https://www.medcentral.com/neurology/migraine/aan-ahs-migraine-guideline-preventive-pharmacologic-treatment-in-adults). (2) **Same pill, new use.** On Sept 10 AbbVie reported a 468-woman Phase 3 trial (LUNA) of atogepant for migraines tied to the menstrual cycle: 1.2 fewer migraine days around the period versus 0.4 on placebo, no new side-effect problems; the company will ask regulators to add it to the label (https://news.abbvie.com/2026-09-10-AbbVie-Extends-Migraine-Leadership-with-Positive-Phase-3-Atogepant-Results-in-Menstrual-Migraine). (3) **The diary beats the barometer.** A Sept 23 study of 53,065 users of a migraine app built a model that called the next day's attack with 91 percent precision (when it said "attack," it was right 91 times in 100) and caught 80 percent of attacks; the last 30 days of a person's own headache history did most of the predicting, with weather a smaller input. It has not yet been shown to help anyone avoid an attack — but it is the case for the family log (https://www.neurologylive.com/view/migraine-forecasting-model-reaches-91-precision-using-app-based-headache-data). (4) **Cannabis, measured.** A Sept 22 review of five controlled trials (1,072 adults) found vaporized THC-plus-CBD gave more people pain relief at two hours than placebo; CBD-only products did not, and the trials were hard to blind (https://themarijuanaherald.com/2026/09/systematic-review-finds-thc-cbd-improves-acute-migraine-outcomes-full-spectrum-cannabis-extract-helps-chronic-back-pain/). (5) **Who to see.** A Sept 4 count found 797 board-certified headache specialists in the whole country and none in 91 percent of counties (https://www.neurologylive.com/view/national-analysis-reveals-certified-headache-specialists-absent-more-than-90-us-counties).
