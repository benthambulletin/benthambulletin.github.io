✶The National Wire✶

1

Tech Stocks Tumble

The tech-heavy Nasdaq index fell 5.5 percent Thursday and the slide spread to Asia and Europe on Friday, the BBC reports, wiping trillions of dollars off big tech companies. Investors fear a Democratic Congress and the states will rein in artificial intelligence and the huge data centers it runs on; NPR puts the U.S. loss at $2 trillion.

2

Judge Pauses Trump's Arch

A federal judge in Washington blocked construction of President Trump's proposed 250-foot triumphal arch for 14 days late Thursday, hours after the federal planning commission for the capital approved it 8–4, CBS reports. Judge Tanya Chutkan sided for now with the Vietnam veterans, the District of Columbia and Virginia who sued, writing that they are likely to show the government skipped required legal steps.

3

California Empties Death Row

Gov. Gavin Newsom says he will commute all 548 death sentences in California to life without parole, CBS reports. The death penalty itself stays on the books, so prosecutors could still seek it in new cases.

4

Los Angeles Changes Mayors

City Councilmember Nithya Raman beat Mayor Karen Bass in Los Angeles, CBS News projects. Raman, whom the BBC calls a democratic socialist, pledged to restore Angelenos' "faith in City Hall."

⛪The Home Wire⛪

1

Rochester · Four New Hall of Fame Toys

The slide, the Pokémon Trading Card Game, the Care Bears and Bingo were inducted into the National Toy Hall of Fame at Rochester's Strong National Museum of Play on Thursday, Rochester First reports. They were chosen from 12 finalists, WXXI reports.

2

Pomfret · Recycling Yard Approved

Pomfret's zoning appeals board granted Steve St. George a special-use permit for a concrete and asphalt recycling yard on Webster Road at its third hearing, Oct. 6, the Observer reports. The recycling equipment can run only on weekdays from 7:30 a.m. to 4 p.m., and no more than 50 days a year.

3

Fredonia · Students Rake the Village

Hundreds of SUNY Fredonia students fan out across the village Saturday from about noon for the annual Fall Sweep, helping residents with yard cleanup and leaves, the Observer reports. Homeowners who want help hang a flier on their door; 17 student clubs have signed up.

★The Marquee★

1

Godzilla Stomps In

Godzilla Minus Zero, Takashi Yamazaki's R-rated new Godzilla film, took in about $6 million in previews before its opening day, Deadline reports. That's ahead of what Predator: Badlands did in previews.

2

Back to Black Is Back

Amy Winehouse's Back to Black returned to the top 10 of four Billboard charts after its 20th-anniversary reissue, including album sales and vinyl, Billboard reports. It also re-entered the all-genre Billboard 200 in 42nd place.

3

Adam Driver Is Done With Star Wars

"The ship has sailed," Adam Driver said of playing Kylo Ren again, the Hollywood Reporter reports: "No more Star Wars." He said Disney turned down The Hunt for Ben Solo, a film he spent two years developing with director Steven Soderbergh, with a short answer: "No, he's dead."
