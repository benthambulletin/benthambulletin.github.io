The Lead

A First 32 at Dawn

The thermometer at Niagara Falls airport, the reading this paper uses for North Tonawanda, stood at 32 under a clear sky at 6:35 this morning. It is the first dawn reading at freezing there this fall; until today the coldest had been 34, on Oct. 21 and 22.

Dunkirk and Scottsville had dawn readings that cold three weeks ago, 28 at Dunkirk and 32 at the Rochester airport on Oct. 21. Both stood at 36 this morning, about the normal low for the date at Buffalo and Rochester.

Late, but it has run later. In 2017 Buffalo and Rochester each went 212 days, from Apr. 8 to Nov. 7, without a freeze, the Northeast Regional Climate Center at Cornell says. That was Rochester's second-longest frost-free season in records back to 1872 and Buffalo's fifth-longest since 1874.

More cold is lined up behind it: rain tonight, rain mixing with snow late Thursday, and a low of 31 on Friday night.

The National Wire

1

Hegseth Impeachment Blocked

The House voted 211–204 on Tuesday to send articles of impeachment, the formal charges that begin the process of removing an official, against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to a committee, which kills the effort for now, CBS and the BBC report. Republican Thomas Massie of Kentucky brought them, accusing Hegseth of waging war on Iran without Congress's approval; Hegseth called it a "farce."

2

Kennedys Sue Over the Arch

Nine members of the Kennedy family sued to stop the 250-foot triumphal arch President Trump plans for Washington, arguing it would block the view of John F. Kennedy's grave and the graves of thousands of veterans at Arlington National Cemetery, the BBC reports. A judge temporarily halted the project last week, and the White House called the family's move "unfortunate."

3

Ebola Passes 11,000 Cases

Congo's Ebola outbreak has reached 11,042 cases and 5,387 deaths, the country's health ministry said Wednesday, NPR reports. It is the deadliest outbreak of the virus Congo has had.

The Home Wire

1

Rochester · County Tax Rate Cut Again

Monroe County Executive Adam Bello proposed a $1.6 billion budget for 2027 that lowers the property tax rate by 25 cents, to an average of $5.42 per $1,000 of assessed value, his seventh cut in a row, WXXI reports. The plan draws $33 million from reserves, and Bello warned that the rising costs of pensions, health care and state and federal mandates "are simply not sustainable over the long term."

2

Dunkirk · Breakwater Finished

The Army Corps of Engineers' Buffalo District finished rebuilding the outer breakwater at Dunkirk Harbor on Oct. 19, the Observer reports. The breakwater is the barrier that protects the harbor and the community around it from Lake Erie.

3

Niagara Falls · A National Hero of the Classroom

Britini D'Angelo, a special education teacher at Henry J. Kalfas Elementary in Niagara Falls, was named Kleenex's National Hero of the Classroom, an award with cash prizes for her and the school, WKBW reports. Diagnosed with autism at 22 months and nonverbal as a small child, she has taught there three years: "This is what I am meant to do for the rest of my life."

The Marquee

1

Stacy Keach Dies at 85

Stacy Keach, the stage actor best known as television's scruffy private eye Mike Hammer, died Tuesday at his home in Poland, CBS reports. His wife said he died "after a long and difficult battle with a serious heart condition and heart failure."

2

Coachella Names Its Headliners

Dua Lipa, Tame Impala and SZA will top the bill at the Coachella festival in Indio, California, on the weekends of April 9–11 and 16–18, 2027, Rolling Stone reports. Madonna plays the Friday under Dua Lipa, and Phoebe Bridgers and Weezer join Tame Impala's Saturday.

3

A Shock on Dancing With the Stars

Jenna Dewan and partner Val Chmerkovskiy were voted off at the end of Tuesday's "Grammy Night," finishing seventh, one round short of the semifinals, Page Six reports. Dewan, a former professional dancer, had been expected to reach the finale; "Honestly, all love," she said.

4

You've Got Mail, Again

Warner Bros. is developing a new You've Got Mail, the Hollywood Reporter says. It will tell a new story rather than remake the 1998 Tom Hanks and Meg Ryan romance, with Jason Orley directing.

The Gridiron

Bills Trade for a Safety

About two hours before Tuesday's deadline the Bills got safety Amani Hooker from Tennessee, sending the Titans a second-round pick in next year's draft and getting a fifth-round pick back, the Associated Press reports. Hooker, in his eighth season, has started since 2021 and already has a career-high five interceptions this year.

Jets Stand Pat

The deadline passed with pass rusher Will McDonald still a Jet, despite his trade request and reported interest from other teams, Gang Green Nation reports. The Jets made no trades on deadline day.
