The Lead

Bills 9–1 for the First Time Since 1991

The Bills beat the Miami Dolphins 26–9 on Sunday in Orchard Park and are 9–1 for the first time since the 1991 season, the Associated Press reports. The defense carried the day: Greg Rousseau had two sacks, including his team-leading 10th of the season, and Miami finished with 185 yards.

The offense sputtered. Josh Allen threw a 4-yard touchdown pass to DJ Moore and two interceptions, and Tyler Bass kicked four field goals, from 54, 43, 40 and 28 yards. James Cook ran for 89 yards and a touchdown, passing 1,000 yards for the fourth time in his five seasons, WIVB reports.

Next is the big one. The Kansas City Chiefs, 8–2, come to Orchard Park on Thanksgiving night at 8:20.

1

Ukraine Says It Sank a Russian Warship Off Libya

President Volodymyr Zelensky says seven Ukrainian naval drones sank the Merkury, one of Russia's newest warships, at the port of Benghazi in Libya on Sunday, and Ukraine's prime minister says a second Russian ship, a cargo vessel carrying weapons, was also hit, the BBC reports. The government that controls eastern Libya confirmed an explosion on the warship while it was docked for fuel and supplies before heading home to Russia.

2

Climate Talks End With No Deal

The United Nations climate talks in Turkey, known as COP31, ended Sunday without a major new agreement after running two days past their Friday deadline, the longest such conference ever held, the BBC reports. The European Union, Pacific island nations and others rejected a final text that never mentioned fossil fuels, the main cause of global warming, and poorer countries objected that it promised no clear money from richer ones to help them adapt.

North Tonawanda · A New Ethics Board

North Tonawanda plans to set up a new ethics board to help city employees work through ethical questions, the Niagara Gazette reports.

Rochester · Five Alarms at the Old Kodak Hawkeye Plant

Rochester firefighters fought a five-alarm fire at the old Kodak Hawkeye Plant until after midnight Monday, with the worst of it on the third floor, Rochester First reports. One firefighter was taken to the hospital, and the cause is under investigation.

5

Chautauqua · Three Schools Vote on Merging

Voters in the Clymer, Sherman and Panama school districts decide on December 15 whether to merge into one district, the Observer reports. A feasibility study found enrollment across the three has dropped 52 percent over the last 30 years.

Tom Jackson Dies at 75

Tom Jackson, a defensive star on the Denver Broncos' "Orange Crush" teams who spent three decades on ESPN's Sunday football shows, has died at 75, his family and the network announced Sunday. He died Friday, but his family asked that the news wait for the start of "Sunday NFL Countdown," where his longtime partner Chris Berman shared it, the Lockport Journal reports.

Hailee Steinfeld Is in the Next "Avengers"

Hailee Steinfeld will return as the archer Kate Bishop in "Avengers: Doomsday," Marvel and Disney Animation announced Sunday in an Instagram post, Variety reports. She made the reveal while promoting Disney's animated "Hexed," joking that "they delivered a chair from a different movie."

Olivia Rodrigo covered the White Stripes' "Dead Leaves and the Dirty Ground" during a small live-to-record set at Jack White's Third Man Records in Nashville on Sunday, Rolling Stone reports. She told the crowd that at 15 she ran a Jack White fan account called "Third Fan Records."

On This Day

November 23, 1936 — Henry Luce put out the first issue of Life, a weekly built on photographs rather than words. He had bought the name from a humor magazine that folded in the Depression. The cover was Margaret Bourke-White's picture of the Fort Peck Dam spillway in Montana, and at its peak its circulation topped 8 million, before television drew away its readers and its advertising and it stopped weekly publication in 1972.
