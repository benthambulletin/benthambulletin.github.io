The National Wire

1

Thanksgiving Storm Knocks Out Power to 800,000

More than 800,000 customers were without power Thursday night across the Midwest and Northeast, more than 170,000 each in Pennsylvania and New Jersey, as a Thanksgiving storm brought torrential rain, high winds and flooding, CBS reports. More than 2,500 flights were delayed and more than 460 canceled, according to FlightAware; New Jersey was under a state of emergency.

2

Strobe Talbott Dies at 80

Strobe Talbott, the Time magazine reporter who became deputy secretary of state under President Bill Clinton and later ran the Brookings Institution, a Washington research group, died Thursday at 80 after a long illness, his son, Devin, confirmed, NPR reports. He and Clinton had roomed together at Oxford, where Time asked Talbott to help translate the leaked memoirs of Soviet leader Nikita Khrushchev.

The Home Wire

3

Rochester · Genesee’s Keg Tree Becomes a Food Drive

The first 200 people who bring three non-perishable food items to the Genesee Brewery’s Keg Tree lighting on Dec. 4 get a free keg-shaped mug etched with the tree, and the food drive runs through the holidays at the Genesee Brew House, WXXI reports. It’s the brewery’s first partnership with Foodlink, which says food-pantry visits in August and September were the highest it has ever seen.

4

Sherman · Village Will Vote on Dissolving

Sherman residents will vote Tuesday, Feb. 9, from 6 a.m. to 9 p.m. at the fire hall on whether to dissolve the village, the Dunkirk Observer reports. Trustees set the date after confirming a citizen petition: 145 of its 151 signatures were from village residents.

5

Silver Creek · Community Bank Branch to Close

Community Bank’s Silver Creek branch, at Main Street and Central Avenue, is set to close in early 2027, Hanover Town Supervisor Lou Pelletter said, the Observer reports. It follows the bank’s plan to close its Central Avenue branch in Dunkirk and move those customers to its Vineyard Drive office on March 19.

Wu-Tang Clan, joined by Red Hot Chili Peppers drummer Chad Smith, played a nearly seven-minute medley at halftime of the Lions’ Thanksgiving game, announced as the wrap-up of the group’s farewell tour, Billboard reports. The Bears won, 39–35, on a touchdown with three seconds left.

The Gridiron

The Lead

Bills 13, Chiefs 10: Bass From 57 at the Gun

Tyler Bass hit a 57-yard field goal as time expired, and the Bills beat the Chiefs 13–10 on a rainy, windy Thanksgiving night in Orchard Park to go 10–1, the best record in the AFC, the AP reports. Bass had tied it at 10 with a 54-yarder early in the fourth; in between, Kansas City’s Harrison Butker missed from 46.

James Cook ran for 117 yards and scored on a 5-yard run, and it was his 10th straight game with at least 75 yards and a touchdown, tying an NFL record Terry Allen set in 1995 and 1996, Buffalo Rumblings reports. Greg Rousseau’s third-quarter sack of Patrick Mahomes was his 43rd, tying Mario Williams for seventh on the Bills’ all-time list.

The passing game didn’t show up: 10 completions in 21 attempts for 81 yards and two interceptions, by Sportradar’s count. The defense held the Chiefs to one touchdown, and replay took away a Rashee Rice catch on third down inside the final two minutes.

On This Day

November 27, 1924 — Macy’s held its first Thanksgiving parade, at 9 a.m., from West 145th Street and Convent Avenue, a six-mile march through Manhattan. It was called the Macy’s Christmas Parade, and it had Mother Goose floats to match the store’s Christmas windows, costumed marchers, Santa Claus and animals borrowed from the Central Park Zoo, with about 10,000 people watching. The balloons didn’t come until 1927. Within days Macy’s announced it would do it again the next year.
