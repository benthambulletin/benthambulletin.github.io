The National Wire

The Lead

Nvidia Takes Its Boss Back, and Trump Posts a Meme

Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive on Monday, less than two weeks after he was forced to resign over revelations of extramarital affairs, one of them with a suspected Chinese spy, the BBC reports. The chipmaker's board had at first turned down his offer to return; President Trump says he "made the deal" to get Huang his job back, and both the president and the company are facing criticism from lawmakers.

Trump also posted the image on today's cover to Truth Social on Monday, with no caption: a doctored version of the Associated Press photo of him after the 2024 shooting at his rally in Butler, Pennsylvania, with Huang in his place, holding up a red bra, GV Wire reports. In a CNBC interview Trump said, "Sex sells, fellas," and in a separate post called Huang "a Great Patriot and National Treasure."

Investors cheered the return. Nvidia's shares jumped 31 percent on Monday, adding almost $1 trillion to its value, the BBC reports.

The Market

S&P 500, daily close through Monday, Nov. 23. Data: Yahoo Finance.

Stocks came roaring back Monday: the S&P 500 rose 4.7 percent and the Nasdaq gained 6.5 percent, CBS reports. Friday's sell-off had pushed the S&P into a correction, a drop of 10 percent or more from its high; Monday's close leaves it about 7 percent below its October 6 peak, out of correction territory for now.

Niagara Falls · Budget Night at City Hall

The Niagara Falls City Council meets today to consider amendments to Mayor Robert Restaino's proposed 2027 budget, the Niagara Gazette reports. The plan totals $107.6 million, $4.1 million more than this year's, with a separate $34.5 million capital budget.

Buffalo · Tops Lawsuit Against Social Media Dismissed

New York's highest court, the Court of Appeals, rejected a lawsuit filed on behalf of victims and families of the 2022 Tops supermarket shooting that argued social media companies helped radicalize the gunman, WIVB reports. The ruling found the platforms are not liable, WGRZ reports.

Webster · The Town vs. the Reindeer

The town of Webster has ordered Dennis McCormick to move the 20-foot inflatable reindeer outside his home on Holt Road, Rochester First reports. He says it's never been an issue, and he's weighing his options.

Adam Driver Joins the X-Men, as the Villain

Adam Driver, Kylo Ren in the last "Star Wars" trilogy, is Marvel's next big villain. He told Seth Meyers about playing Dr. Nathaniel Essex, better known as Mister Sinister, a hyperintelligent, shape-shifting telepath and the main villain in the upcoming "X-Men" film, Variety reports.

Hila Klein Leaves the "H3 Podcast"

Hila Klein is leaving the "H3 Podcast," a month after it came out that her husband and co-host, Ethan Klein, had a six-month affair, Variety reports. "I'm devastated that things have come to this," she said in a post on the show's Instagram.

The Gridiron

Bills: The One Seed Is in Reach

With the Jaguars and Bengals both losing this week, the Bills can lock up the AFC's top seed, which has eluded them throughout Josh Allen's career, by winning the rest of their conference games, Buffalo Rumblings reports. The Bengals fell 22–20 to the Commanders on a 26-yard field goal in the final seconds Monday night, ProFootballTalk reports. Before Thursday's game, the team will honor restaurateur Russell Salvatore, a longtime supporter, WGRZ reports.

Jets: A Win, and an Argument

Sunday's win in Los Angeles was the Jets' first in five weeks, and Gang Green Nation reports the fan base is split between enjoying it and worrying about draft position. Next: at Miami on Sunday.

On This Day

November 24, 1971 — A man who bought his ticket as "Dan Cooper" hijacked Northwest Orient Flight 305 between Portland and Seattle, traded the passengers for $200,000 in twenty-dollar bills and four parachutes, and jumped from the back of the plane into the dark somewhere between Seattle and Reno. The "D.B." was a mistake by the press that stuck. In 1980 a boy found $5,800 of the ransom, rotting in a package, with serial numbers that matched. The FBI weighed more than 800 suspects, never named him, and moved its resources to other cases in 2016.
