The Lead

Microsoft Outage Knocks Out Outlook and Teams

A Microsoft outage that has dragged on for hours has left Outlook email, the Teams meeting app and the Copilot assistant down or spotty for users from Asia to the United States, hitting companies and some government offices, CBS reports.

Microsoft says the outage is hitting the programs that depend on Exchange Online, the service that runs its email. The company is still working to pin down the cause and says it has no sign of an attack.

Early Friday the company said service was coming back gradually, starting in Asia, with mailbox access improving, but warned that "some users may continue to experience intermittent issues." If work email is slow this morning, this may be why.

Update: Brooklyn Toll Rises to Eight

Eight people died and 18 were injured in Wednesday's building collapse in Brooklyn, the BBC reports; two of the dead were children, according to PBS. The search ended Thursday, about 24 hours after the collapse, when fire officials determined there were no more victims.

2

A Challenge to Schumer

Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, whose aide said last week he would not run, said Thursday he will challenge New York's Sen. Chuck Schumer for Senate majority leader, the top job in the chamber Democrats won in last week's midterms, CBS reports. Van Hollen wrote that "we need new leadership," and Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont quickly backed him; Schumer answered, "I have the votes," with the vote expected early next month.

The Home Wire

1

Rochester · A Founder Steps Down After 45 Years

Nydia Padilla-Rodriguez, who founded Borinquen Dance Theatre, retires in December after 45 years as its artistic director, WXXI reports. She built an award-winning youth program that teaches self-confidence and discipline through dance while celebrating Puerto Rican culture; her successor grew up in Rochester.

2

Fredonia · Borrowing for the Water Hookup

Fredonia is weighing a bond anticipation note, a short-term loan taken out ahead of a longer bond, to help pay for connecting to the North County Water District, Trustee Tim Mulholland said this week, the Observer reports. With the project on pause, the first step would be repaying the village water fund about $500,000 already spent on engineering and related costs; the note could be ready by December.

3

Buffalo · Catholic Health Staff to Mask Up

Starting Monday, Catholic Health, the hospital system, will require doctors, nurses and other staff who care for patients to wear masks, and will strongly encourage visitors to, as flu cases climb across the state, WKBW reports. "This is very different than, for example, masking in public the way we did with COVID," said Dr. Thomas Cumbo, an infectious disease physician at Kenmore Mercy.

The Marquee

1

Dolly's Last Song

U2's new album, Carnaval de Luz, out today, closes with "Torn," a duet between Bono and Dolly Parton that is her final recording, Rolling Stone reports. Parton, who died in August at 80, recorded her part in June; "she got out of her bed to sing this song," Bono said.

2

Rosalía Makes It Three

Rosalía won Album of the Year for Lux at Thursday's Latin Grammys, her third time taking the night's top prize and the first time a woman has won it that often as a lead artist, Rolling Stone reports. She was in tears as she accepted it from the Colombian rocker Juanes, who helped her early in her career.

3

Vader's Lightsaber: $4.875 Million

The lightsaber Darth Vader used in The Empire Strikes Back and Return of the Jedi, including the duel where he tells Luke Skywalker "I am your father," sold Thursday for $4.875 million, CBS reports. Heritage Auctions calls it a record for any Star Wars collectible.

4

Danes and McGregor, Jan. 13

The Spot, a psychological thriller starring Claire Danes and Ewan McGregor about a successful surgeon and her schoolteacher husband, premieres on Hulu on Jan. 13, Deadline reports. Hulu released the first photos along with the date.

The Gridiron

Bills: Brown Sits Out

Right tackle Spencer Brown did not practice Thursday because of the ankle injury he suffered Monday night in Minnesota, Buffalo Rumblings reports. Sam Franklin was added to the injury report with a shoulder injury.

Jets Want Geno Back

The Jets are "expected to seriously consider" bringing quarterback Geno Smith back in 2027, The Athletic's Zack Rosenblatt wrote, according to Gang Green Nation. Smith, who can become a free agent after the season, has played well and is respected in the locker room, the site says.

UB Bulls: Five Straight, and Bowl Eligible

The University at Buffalo beat Ball State 28–7 on Wednesday night for its fifth win in a row. It was the Bulls' sixth win, which makes them bowl eligible, meaning they have the six wins a team needs to be invited to a postseason bowl game, WGRZ reports. They did it on the ground: 302 rushing yards on 52 carries and three rushing touchdowns, one a 67-yard run in the fourth quarter. Ball State's only points came on a blocked punt, and it missed three field goals. Central Michigan visits Wednesday night.
