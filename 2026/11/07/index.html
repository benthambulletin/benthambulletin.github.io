The Lead

Lake Erie Sets a Record for Early November

Lake Erie hit 60 degrees off Buffalo on Thursday, WKBW reports, two degrees above the old record for the date and even with the warmest November reading on the books, set Nov. 1–3. It was 59 on Friday, the National Weather Service says, another record for the date; normal for early November is about 53. Lake Ontario was 58 off Rochester.

What it means for winter. Lake-effect snow comes from bitter air crossing warm water, and the bigger the gap, the heavier the bands. A 60-degree lake is a loaded battery: the first real arctic blast could set off an unusually intense storm. Until that cold arrives, early bands tend to fall as rain or slush, and no long cold spell shows up in the forecast models yet, WBLK notes. A lake this warm also freezes late, or barely at all, which can keep lake-effect going deep into winter.

Where it lands. Which towns get buried depends on the wind. One direction aims the bands at the Southtowns, the scene on the cover; swing it a little and they land on Chautauqua County's hills, Dunkirk and Fredonia included. North Tonawanda gets the big totals only when the wind lines up just so.

A New Chief of Staff

President Trump named longtime aide Stephen Miller his White House chief of staff, the president's top aide who runs the White House, on Friday, replacing Susie Wiles in the biggest shake-up of his team since Republicans' midterm losses, CBS reports. "I wanted to make a change," the president said, calling Miller "one of my most trusted advisors."

2

A Better Jobs Month

Employers added 165,000 jobs in October, more than expected, PBS NewsHour reports, and the August and September numbers were revised up by a combined 84,000. The good news comes as questions grow about how long the boom in artificial-intelligence spending can last.

3

The Pope Travels After Surgery

Pope Leo XIV, 71, told reporters he is "in pretty good health" as he began a 12-day Latin America tour, three weeks after surgery for lung cancer, the BBC reports. The trip runs through Uruguay and Argentina to Peru, where he lived and worked for years, which the Vatican calls a "homecoming."

⛪The Home Wire⛪

1

Rochester · Flu Comes Early

Flu cases across New York jumped 77 percent in a week, to 7,696, the state Health Department says, and Monroe County is seeing an early-season surge, Rochester First reports. The county has logged 243 cases so far this season.

2

Dunkirk · The Fire Chief on Battery Farms

Battery energy storage systems, big banks of batteries that soak up power from the grid and release it when demand is high, carry "unique hazards," Dunkirk Fire Chief Nicholas Barter told Revitalize Dunkirk, the Observer reports. "Everything has risk," he said; the department is working with developers to plan for them before one arrives.

3

Newfane · 67 Years Behind the Chair

Bob Eadie put away his clippers for good on Oct. 30 after 67 years as Newfane's barber, the Lockport Journal reports. For generations of families, a trip to his shop was a chance to catch up and swap stories as much as a haircut.

Céline Dion, Metalhead

Céline Dion was caught on fan video playing air bass from a suite at Metallica's residency at the Sphere in Las Vegas on Thursday, Billboard reports. Slash and members of No Doubt were in the suite too, Billboard says; Dion has said Metallica is one of her twins' favorite bands.

Knicks guard Tyler Kolek called Taylor Swift a "very performative" fan, meaning one who shows up to be seen, Deadline reports. The reigning NBA champions took her side within hours: a team spokesperson thanked the 14-time Grammy winner for "many years" of support.
