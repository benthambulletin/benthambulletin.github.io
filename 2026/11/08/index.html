How the NASCAR Chase Ends

An AI wrote this section.

What changed. NASCAR scrapped its old elimination playoffs, which ended with four drivers racing one final race where the best finisher took the title, before this season, and brought back "the Chase," Fox Sports reports. After the regular season the top 16 drivers had their points reset, with 100 points separating first from 16th, then ran 10 races in which every point counted and nobody was knocked out. Today's race at Homestead-Miami Speedway, a 1.5-mile oval near Miami, is the 10th and last.

The math. Only three drivers can still win, NBC Sports says: Kyle Larson with 2,469 points, Christopher Bell 67 behind and Denny Hamlin 70 behind. A race win pays 55 points, each of the race's two "stages" (checkpoints at set laps) pays up to 10 to the top ten, and the fastest lap pays one, so 76 is the most anyone can earn today. Larson clinches with just 9: a top-three run at the end of Stage 1 on lap 80 would do it, and with no stage points at all, finishing 28th or better would. Bell and Hamlin both have to win the race, pile up stage points and need Larson's day to fall apart.

What it means. For a casual fan, the change means a title earned over ten races rather than decided by one, and the math shows why today may not be close: Larson needs 9 points, Bell and Hamlin need a win and help. If he holds on, it is his third Cup title, after 2021 and 2025, Fox says; neither Bell nor Hamlin has won one.

Coming down the road. The green flag drops shortly after 3 p.m. on NBC, with pre-race coverage at 2, while the Jets are still playing in Kansas City. Monday's paper will have the champion, and The Chase goes quiet until next season's Daytona 500.

✶The National Wire✶

The Lead

Trump Offers Dana White the Podium

President Trump said Saturday he has offered the job of White House press secretary, the president's chief spokesman who briefs reporters, to UFC boss Dana White, NPR reports. "Dana, please take the job!" he posted.

White, in London promoting a boxing match, said he was flying to Washington and would meet with Trump on Monday. Asked how he would fit it in with running the UFC, the mixed-martial-arts league, he said, "Yeah, I got a lot of jobs already." He also posted a photo of himself at the briefing-room podium, captioned "On to the next chapter."

The job has been open since Oct. 16, when Katie Zacharia was removed after five days, CBS says; Karoline Leavitt left it in August. At least one outlet suggested the offer might be tongue-in-cheek, and the White House did not comment.

"Dana will be a Press Secretary like none other," Trump wrote.

Update: Who Leads the New Senate?

Democrats will hold at least 54 Senate seats when Congress convenes in January, CBS reports, and several incoming senators want someone other than Chuck Schumer, who has led Senate Democrats since 2017, as majority leader, the senator who decides what comes up for a vote. "There should be no coronation," said Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen, whose aide says he will not run for the job himself.

3

Six Killed in Krakow

A 25-year-old gunman with far-right views drove into the Main Square of Krakow, Poland's second-largest city, on Saturday and fired at random, killing six people and wounding 15 before police shot him within three minutes, AP and the BBC report. Police later found three of his family members dead at his home; Poland's defense minister called it "an act of terror."

⛪The Home Wire⛪

Rochester · A Perfect 900

Steve Meyer bowled three straight 300 games, twelve strikes in a row each time, at Domm's Bowling Center on Saturday for a perfect 900 series, Rochester First reports. He is only the third bowler in Upstate New York to do it.

2

Bemus Point · Veterans Charity Fights Fraud Charges

The operators of Healing for Heroes, a retreat for disabled veterans, are fighting charges Attorney General Letitia James filed in August, the Dunkirk Observer reports. She says the couple who run it rented the 30-acre property on Airbnb at least 32 times in 2024 and 2025, kept the $33,312 for themselves, and spent nearly half of about $100,000 in donations on their property.

3

Grand Island & Buffalo · The State Sues Over Seneca Land

New York has filed lawsuits against the U.S. Interior Department over its decisions giving Seneca Nation land in Buffalo and on Grand Island "restricted-fee" status, WIVB reports. That federal protection takes the land out from under state oversight, the state argues, WGRZ says.
