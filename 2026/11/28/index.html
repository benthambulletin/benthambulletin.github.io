The Lead

Andrea Stewart-Cousins, Senate Leader, Dies at 76

Andrea Stewart-Cousins, the Yonkers Democrat who led the New York State Senate and was the first Black woman to lead a legislative conference in Albany, died unexpectedly on Friday, WXXI reports. She was 76, and the cause wasn’t immediately clear.

She held one of the three most powerful posts in state government and had a hand in all the major legislation passed since Democrats took the Senate in 2018, WXXI reports. Senate Democrats had voted to give her another two-year term as majority leader less than two weeks ago.

Senate Democrats must now pick a new leader by majority vote before the 2027 session begins, Rochester First reports, and her Westchester seat will need a special election.

Two Jets Clip Wings at Reagan National

Two American Airlines Boeing 737s clipped wings while taxiing for departure at Reagan National Airport outside Washington on Friday evening; no one was hurt, and the Federal Aviation Administration will investigate, the BBC reports. It’s the same airport where 67 people died when a jetliner and an Army helicopter collided in January 2025.

3

Blast Near a School in Afghanistan

At least seven students were killed and 99 hurt Saturday morning in an explosion near a school in Asadabad, the capital of Kunar province, a Taliban government spokesman said, the BBC reports. Initial findings blame “negligence and carelessness” at an explosives depot belonging to nearby mining companies.

Lancaster and Hamburg · The Lights Are On

Legendary Lights, nearly 400 decorated homes in Stony Brook, a Lancaster neighborhood (not the Long Island one), lit up Thanksgiving night and runs every night until New Year’s Day; drive or walk through, WIVB and WGRZ report. The Festival of Lights at the Hamburg Fairgrounds opened Friday with more than 70 drive-through displays and runs through Dec. 30.

4

Lewiston · A Credit Union Moves In

Ontario Shores Federal Credit Union plans to open its fifth location at 451 Center St. in Lewiston, the Niagara Gazette reports. “We think it’s a great opportunity for the credit union and the people of Lewiston,” CEO Jeff Bell said, calling it a not-for-profit, community-based option.

5

Buffalo · UB Is Going to the MAC Title Game

The University at Buffalo beat Akron 17–9 on Friday to clinch a spot in the Mid-American Conference championship game, WGRZ reports. Messiah Burch ran for 130 yards and a touchdown on 24 carries.

6

Rochester · Cruel Summer Comes Home

Cruel Summer, a Rochester-based Taylor Swift tribute band that has spent the year selling out venues across the Northeast, is coming home for its annual Swift birthday show, Rochester First reports. It plays Saturday, Dec. 12, at the Theater at Innovative Field.

WWE’s Bronson Reed Dies at 38

Jermaine Haley, the Australian wrestler known in WWE as Bronson Reed, died Friday of medical complications, WWE said, the Hollywood Reporter reports; he leaves a pregnant wife, Paige, and a daughter. Us Weekly reports he had suffered broken ribs and a lacerated kidney when he was thrown off the stage at WWE’s Crown Jewel event in Saudi Arabia on Nov. 7.

The Killers’ Guitarist Survived a Major Stroke

Dave Keuning, 50, says he had a major stroke two weeks ago, went straight into surgery thanks to his wife acting fast, and that doctors gave him a 40% chance of survival, Billboard reports. “By some miracle I am fine,” he wrote, adding that the first hour after a stroke is critical.

Bob Newhart Couldn’t Afford to Quit

Michael Seth Starr’s biography of Bob Newhart says he once tried to quit “The Bob Newhart Show” and backed down when CBS threatened a $6 million lawsuit, Page Six reports. “I don’t have $6 million,” he said later. “And my wife wouldn’t lend me any of her money.”

On This Day

November 28, 1925 — A 77-year-old fiddler named Uncle Jimmy Thompson played on WSM, a Nashville radio station barely seven weeks old, when George D. Hay put him on the air for the first WSM Barn Dance. Hay had come from Chicago’s National Barn Dance and copied the idea: country music, live, on Saturday nights. Two years later, coming on right after an NBC classical music program, Walter Damrosch’s Music Appreciation Hour, Hay told listeners that from then on they would hear the “Grand Ole Opry,” and the name stuck. It passed its 4,000th straight Saturday night broadcast in 2002 and is still on the air.
