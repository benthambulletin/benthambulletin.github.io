The Lead

Wall Street Slides Into a Correction

The stock market fell hard again Friday. The S&P 500, an index of 500 big American companies that most retirement funds follow, dropped 3.7 percent, its seventh losing day in a row and its longest such streak since February 2020, CBS reports. That left it more than 10 percent below its recent high, which is what Wall Street calls a correction.

S&P 500, daily close, through Thursday, Nov. 19, the day before Friday's fall.

The worry is artificial intelligence. A.I. companies drove much of this year's gains, and investors now doubt that the enormous spending on data centers will pay off; chip makers led Friday's losses. The Nasdaq, the index heaviest in technology, lost 1,261 points and gave back everything it had gained in 2026. Anthropic, the A.I. company that began trading Thursday, fell 9.3 percent to $88.14, still above the $83 its shares were first sold for. (A disclosure: this paper is put together with Anthropic's A.I.)

For a family, it means a 401(k) or an index fund is probably worth less than it was at its October peak. It does not say what comes next: an analyst at E*Trade told CBS that only five of the last 15 corrections from record highs since 1983 went on to become bear markets, meaning falls of 20 percent or more.

"It's impossible to know when any selloff will run its course."
— Chris Larkin, E*Trade from Morgan Stanley

Guilty Verdict in the Palisades Fire

A jury has found Jonathan Rinderknecht guilty of starting the blaze that grew into the Palisades Fire in Los Angeles in January 2025, CBS reports; an earlier trial ended with a deadlocked jury. The fire killed 12 people and destroyed more than 6,800 buildings, and he faces between five and 40 years in federal prison.

2

A $7.25 Billion Settlement Over Roundup

A Missouri judge on Friday approved a $7.25 billion settlement of lawsuits claiming the weedkiller Roundup causes cancer, the BBC reports. Bayer, the German company that makes it, will pay users who have non-Hodgkin lymphoma, a blood cancer, and those who fall ill later; payments run from $10,000 to $165,000.

3

Update: A Court Halts the Iowa Blast

A federal appeals court on Friday put the Army's plan to blow up unstable explosives at an Iowa ammunition plant on hold indefinitely, CBS reports. Two environmental groups had sued, arguing that the emergency permit was not valid and that residents had too little warning.

The Home Wire

1

Rochester · Comfort Care for the Zoo's Elephant

Genny C., the African elephant who has lived at the Seneca Park Zoo since 1979, is receiving palliative care, meaning treatment aimed at keeping her comfortable as she ages, WXXI reports. The zoo says she has had digestive trouble for months and has struggled to keep her weight, and that the barn will be closed to visitors on days she is not feeling well.

2

Rochester · No Border Patrol Station Downtown

U.S. Customs and Border Protection has dropped its plan to turn space in the Keating Federal Building in downtown Rochester into a Border Patrol station, WXXI reports. Sens. Chuck Schumer and Kirsten Gillibrand and Rep. Joseph Morelle announced the reversal Friday; the agency says it will look at other options.

3

Portland · A Winemaker Earns a Rare Diploma

Brian Barmore, head winemaker at 21 Brix Winery in Portland, in Chautauqua County, has earned the top diploma of the Wine & Spirit Education Trust along with its certificate of merit, the Observer reports. He first walked into the winery as a customer in 2018; his final paper was on orange wines.

The Marquee

1

Patrick Stewart's Cause of Death

Patrick Stewart, the English actor who died Oct. 28 at 86, died of cardiopulmonary arrest, with Lewy body dementia listed as an underlying condition, Rolling Stone reports, citing a death certificate obtained by People. The statement announcing his death had spoken of "a brief and poignant journey" with Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia.

2

Ina Garten Will Not Be Dancing

Ina Garten, the Barefoot Contessa, said on her podcast that she was asked to do Dancing With the Stars and turned it down, E! News reports. "Nobody needs to see that," she said. "That's not happening."

3

How Cher Got Phil Collins's Line

Phil Collins says Cher sang his line in the "We Are the World" finale at Live Aid in 1985 because he was too tired to stay for it, Billboard reports. The two had met that day on the Concorde he took to play both the London and Philadelphia shows; she asked if he could get her in, and he told her, "You're Cher. ... Just turn up."

The Gridiron

Bills: Dolphins Tomorrow at 1

The Bills are 8–1, the best record in the AFC, and over the last three games have allowed one touchdown and 28 points in all, the Associated Press reports in the Dunkirk Observer. Asked whether that is because they keep drawing backup quarterbacks, with another due Sunday, defensive tackle Ed Oliver said: "Who cares. ... You don't get to choose."

Jets: Smith Is Cleared

Geno Smith practiced in full Friday and carries no injury label into Sunday's game with the Chargers, ProFootballTalk reports. "He looks solid," coach Aaron Glenn said. "It's very encouraging."

Around the Division: Maye Is Hurt

Patriots quarterback Drake Maye broke his right fibula, a bone in the lower leg, in last week's loss to the Lions in Munich and is likely out for most or all of the regular season, ProFootballTalk says, citing ESPN. He has no ligament damage, and would likely return only if New England is still in the playoff hunt.
