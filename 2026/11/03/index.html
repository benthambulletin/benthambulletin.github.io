✶The National Wire✶

1

U.S. Strikes an Iranian Island

The U.S. struck Iran's Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow channel out of the Persian Gulf, after Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired on a U.S. aircraft, U.S. officials told CBS; they called the strikes limited and defensive. Iran's Guard said it shot down three U.S. drones, the BBC reports, as mediators work to keep the war that began in February from flaring again after today's vote.

2

Ukraine: 29 Dead at an Aid Site

A Russian attack on a site where people had gathered for humanitarian aid in Ukraine's southern Zaporizhzhia region killed 29, with 48 still missing, NPR reports. Officials say at least nine 3,000-kilogram bombs hit the village, the BBC reports, and overnight strikes also hit power plants and hospitals across the country.

3

Court Won't Stop California's Count

The Supreme Court on Monday turned down the Trump administration's request to order California election officials to set aside mail ballots from 20,000 people it alleges are not citizens, CBS reports. The administration filed the request only hours earlier, on the eve of today's vote; the ruling lets California keep counting those ballots.

⛪The Home Wire⛪

1

Rochester · Veterans Day Parade Saturday

Monroe County's fifth annual Veterans Day Parade steps off at 10:30 a.m. Saturday from South Goodman Street and Highland Avenue and ends with a ceremony at the Vietnam veterans memorial in Gary Beikirch Park, WXXI reports. Marching bands, veterans' groups and active-duty service members will march; State Senate Minority Leader Robert Ortt, a North Tonawanda Republican and Army National Guard veteran, is grand marshal.

2

Dunkirk · $21 Million for the Schools

The Dunkirk City School District awarded 24 bids worth more than $21 million for what the superintendent calls "the biggest phase" of its building project, the Observer reports. It covers a renovated cafeteria and kitchen at the middle and high school, a refurbished football field, track and tennis courts with new scoreboards, and air conditioning in classrooms.

3

Tonawanda · Flood Loans Open

The U.S. Small Business Administration has opened an office at the Ellwood Volunteer Firehouse in the Town of Tonawanda to help people hit by September's record flash flooding in North Buffalo and the Northtowns apply for low-interest disaster loans, WKBW reports. Homeowners, renters, nonprofits and small businesses can apply, with rates starting at 3 percent.

★The Marquee★

1

More Cuts Coming at Disney

Disney is preparing another round of layoffs that would reach thousands of workers across its TV networks, film studio and other divisions, Deadline reports, citing sources. Some divisions are looking for up to 20 percent in cost savings.

2

Elsinore Leads the British Indies

Simon Stone's Elsinore, starring Andrew Scott, leads the British Independent Film Award nominations with 12, the Hollywood Reporter reports. Clio Barnard's I See Buildings Fall Like Lightning has eight, and Animol, the first feature directed by Adolescence actor Ashley Walters, has seven.

3

The Chicks Push Back a Show

The Chicks postponed Monday's concert at Hollywood's Dolby Theatre hours before doors opened, citing illness, and moved it to Wednesday, Billboard reports. The show is part of their tour marking 20 years of Taking the Long Way.
