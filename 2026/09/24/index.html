Outdoor window Wide open again, and it holds all the way to Wednesday, which touches 74. Seven days without a drop of rain in the forecast. Saturday at 71 is the pick for anything that needs doing outside. Pollen Ragweed high.
The nor'easter: Long Island's weekend, hour by hour. The storm is strengthening off the coast this morning and turning north. About eighteen million people from Maine to Florida are under coastal alerts.

Today — partly sunny and windy on Long Island, upper sixties, northeast gusts 30 to 35 and 39 on the east end, higher offshore. Coastal flooding at high tide. Friday — cloudy, windy, showers arriving, minor to moderate flooding on the high tides; this is when it turns. Saturday and Sunday are the worst of it — periods of moderate rain, gusts over 40 and possibly 50, mainly in Suffolk County, with scattered power outages considered likely. Under an inch of rain in most scenarios; the wind and the water are the problem, not the rain. It tapers Sunday night.

Saturday's full moon puts astronomical high tides underneath all of it, which is why the flooding forecast is worse than the rain forecast. The track is still the variable: a shift west means heavier rain and stronger wind for Nassau and Suffolk; a shift east means wind and beach damage without the soaking. Nobody east of Albany should plan anything outdoors Saturday.

🍂The Turning🍂

Still at the starting line out here. The spotters have Niagara, Chautauqua and Monroe counties at essentially no change, with a trace of color logged near Niagara Falls and five percent down in Geneseo. The pattern this week — sun all day, mid-forties at night — is what moves those numbers, so next Wednesday's report should look different.
The Adirondacks are at thirty-five percent already. Color starts in the mountains and walks south and west; the Southern Tier goes before the lake plain does, because the lakes hold heat into October and keep the trees green a week or two longer than the hills.
